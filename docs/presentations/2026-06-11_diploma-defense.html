A System for Automatic Normalization of Surzhyk and Dialectal Forms of the Ukrainian Language

«Lexicon»: a system that translates four non-standard varieties of Ukrainian into the literary norm.

Denys KovalStudent, group ШІ-42
Oleksii SyvokonSupervisor
Computer Science · 122Dept. of Artificial Intelligence Systems
Problem

Four non-standard varieties of Ukrainian

🗣️ Surzhyk Ukrainian-Russian mixed speech, common across the country
🏔️ Hutsul Archaic Carpathian dialect with Polish and Romanian influences
⛰️ Boiko Mountain dialect with Polish and Old Slavic influences
🇭🇺 Transcarpathian Shaped by Hungarian, Slovak and Romanian

Open data is scarce: one small parallel corpus exists for Hutsul, none for Boiko or Transcarpathian. LanguageTool and similar tools treat these varieties as spelling errors; Vuyko Mistral covers Hutsul only.

Goal: translate all four into literary Ukrainian automatically, and check whether small fine-tuned models can compete with commercial LLMs here.
Research Framework

Goal and scope of the work

Goal

Develop a system that automatically normalizes surzhyk and three regional dialects (Hutsul, Boiko, Transcarpathian) to the literary standard of modern Ukrainian, using neural models and low-resource data techniques.

Object

The process of automatic normalization of non-standard forms of modern Ukrainian - surzhyk and regional dialects.

Subject

Methods of building parallel datasets for low-resource language varieties, and methods of training translation models and LLMs for normalization.

Scientific novelty

First single system covering surzhyk + three dialects; first public synthetic parallel datasets for Boiko and Transcarpathian; comparative evaluation of a translation model vs. an LLM on multi-dialect normalization.

Practical value

The «Lexicon» web application: normalizes four varieties of Ukrainian to the literary standard and supports voice input.

Approach

Approach

Given

9,853 manual Hutsul pairs · normative sentences from Spivavtor · scanned dialect & surzhyk dictionaries (PDF)

Find

A single system that normalizes text from any of the four varieties to the literary standard

Methods

Synthetic data expansion (rules + LLM) · fine-tuning translation models · prompt engineering

Result

«Lexicon» web app: pick a variety, type or speak, get normalized literary Ukrainian

We generate pairs in the direction norm → non-standard: the literary side comes from a verified corpus, so it stays correct.
Data

Corpus synthesis: two pipelines

Rule-based - surzhyk 20,911 pairs

  • Phase 1: phrase-level substitution via regular expressions
  • Phase 2: word-level substitution with pymorphy2 lemmatization, preserving case, gender and number agreement
  • Deterministic and dictionary-driven; needs no manual annotation

LLM-generated - dialects 108,812 pairs

  • TF-IDF cosine similarity selects the 10 nearest dictionary entries per sentence
  • Mistral-Small-24B (4-bit, local inference), batches of 5 sentences, JSON output
  • Quality control: double parsing, filtering of incomplete outputs, deduplication

Train/validation split 90/10. The test set is fully manual: 200 pairs, 50 per variety, with no overlap with training data.

Models

Two models

umt5-base encoder-decoder · ≈300M params

  • Full fine-tuning, one model for all four varieties
  • Variety prefix in the input: "Translate from Hutsul: …"
  • 40 epochs · lr 5×10⁻⁵ · label smoothing 0.1 · early stopping (patience 5)
  • Decoding: beam search

MamayLM decoder-only · Gemma-3-4B · ≈4B params

  • QLoRA: base weights frozen in 4-bit, only low-rank adapters trained (r=16, α=32: ≈13M weights, 300× fewer)
  • Variety marker in the system role of the chat template
  • 3 epochs · lr 2×10⁻⁴ · Unsloth acceleration
  • Decoding: sampling, τ=0.1, top-k 25

We also tried mBART, ByT5 and Lapa LLM; umt5-base and MamayLM scored best in the initial Hutsul-only baseline runs.

Models

Training umt5-base

314k steps over the multidialect corpus; training loss settles near 1.9, validation BLEU near 71.7.

We kept umt5-base as the main model: it is 13× smaller than MamayLM, scores higher on three of the four varieties, and responds faster. The web app runs on it.

Results

Hypotheses and outcomes

H1 Fine-tuned model reaches chrF++ ≥ 85 on surzhyk Confirmed

Both of our models passed the threshold: umt5-base - 93.58, MamayLM - 91.22.

H2 Fine-tuned models vs commercial LLMs Confirmed*

+6.91 chrF++ on surzhyk and +21.56 on Hutsul over the strongest zero-shot baseline. Fine-tuning lifted MamayLM itself by +25.64 (surzhyk) and +57.29 (Hutsul). *Holds for varieties with quality training data.

H3 Average chrF++ ≥ 75 across all four varieties Not confirmed

Average: 68.69. Boiko (66.82) and Transcarpathian (27.45) train on synthetic data only; the commercial models stay ahead there.

Application

The «Lexicon» web app

Stack

NiceGUI + FastAPI; the interface is ≈250 lines of Python.

Normalizer

Fine-tuned umt5-base, beam search with 5 beams, float16 on GPU.

Voice input

ASR module speechbrain/m-ctc-t-large; WebM, OGG and WAV are converted to 16 kHz mono.

UX

Four variety presets with descriptions and one example sentence per variety.

Conclusions

Conclusions

  1. Assembled a parallel corpus for surzhyk and three dialects: 125,615 / 13,756 / 200 pairs from two synthesis pipelines (rules with pymorphy2; Mistral-Small-24B). For Boiko and Transcarpathian this appears to be the first public parallel data.
  2. Fine-tuned two models of different architectures: umt5-base (full fine-tuning) and MamayLM / Gemma-3-4B (QLoRA, 300× fewer trainable weights).
  3. On surzhyk and Hutsul the fine-tuned models scored above the zero-shot commercial LLMs: chrF++ 93.58 vs 86.67 and 86.90 vs 65.34.
  4. Where training data was synthetic only (Boiko, Transcarpathian), the commercial LLMs stayed ahead; manual annotation quality definitely matters more than data volume.
  5. Built the «Lexicon» web application: four varieties, normalization with umt5-base and voice input through an ASR module.
